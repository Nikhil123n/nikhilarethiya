# Art Direction

## Identity

Treat the Professional Experience road as a hand-drawn documentary, not a product-marketing timeline. Preserve the portfolio's tactile sketchbook language: warm paper, ink, pencil, imperfect road marks, spiral and notebook cues, restrained annotation, and typography already established by the site.

The visual hierarchy must begin with real content:

1. role and organization
2. time and place in the career chronology
3. concrete impact or shipped outcome
4. supporting technologies and tags
5. reflective takeaway

Motion and ornament may reveal these facts, but must not replace or obscure them.

## One signature element

Choose one memorable device for the entire road. Examples include a route line that gathers evidence, a traveler's field-note marker, or a single artifact that evolves at each stop. The signature element should connect every chapter and culminate in a clear final state.

Do not give every stop an unrelated visual trick. At any moment, one stop should be dominant, adjacent stops should provide context, and completed stops should visibly recede.

## Content-specific visual opportunities

Use these only when they truthfully correspond to the underlying experience data:

- NIC: a district or service grid drawn as civic infrastructure.
- SNDL: a neural or signal pipeline that makes model flow legible.
- UN: a validation ledger, evidence stamp, or restrained globe/network trace.
- HIVE: a glucose waveform, closed-loop signal, or care pathway.

These are narrative diagrams, not decorative icons. Label them with real outcomes and keep them understandable without motion.

## Anti-generic constraints

Avoid:

- generic SaaS gradients, glass cards, neon glows, or dashboard chrome
- gratuitous 3D, Rive, particles, or parallax without a story function
- equal visual weight for every stop
- decorative charts that do not encode real information
- generic timeline dots connected by a line as the sole concept
- multiple signature interactions competing in one viewport
- fake metrics, invented quotes, or placeholder career claims

Use novelty through composition, pacing, and content transformation rather than an accumulation of effects.
